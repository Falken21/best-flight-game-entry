# Isla Brava: Water Bomber — design brief

**Player promise.** You are the only firebomber pilot on Isla Brava. When the
pine forest catches, you skim the crater lake, haul a tank of water up the
volcano's shoulder and knock the fire down before the island burns.

**Primary verb.** Scoop low, drop true. The whole game is the circuit:
descend to the lake, skim to fill the tank, climb heavy, line up on a smoke
column, release over the flames.

**Core loop contract.** Scoop (risk: the lowest, fastest pass of the run,
beside rising terrain) → climb (a full tank makes the plane heavy and slow to
climb) → drop (a projected impact marker shows where the water will land;
a direct hit douses, a near miss halves the fire). The reward changes state:
a doused fire stops spreading and stops feeding the island's SCORCH meter.
What repeats every 5–30 seconds: one scoop-and-drop circuit per fire, while
the spread timer forces a triage decision — which fire is closest to the
village, the lighthouse or the fuel depot?

**Escalation.** Each wave ignites more fires and the wind picks up: smoke
leans, spread leans with it, and fires start closer to the buildings. A fire
that reaches a building burns it down: +20% SCORCH at a stroke.

**Fail states.** SCORCH reaches 100% (the island is lost), terrain strike,
or ditching in the sea at speed. A gentle water touchdown at scoop speed on
the lake is the one safe splash.

**Screenshot plan.** (1) Cover: chase view lining up on a burning ridge, the
pulsing target ring over the fire, lake and volcano behind, HUD lit.
(2) The scoop: skimming the lake at speed, spray flying, tank filling.
(3) Title over a golden-hour vista of the island. (4) The drop: the cascade
mid-fall, steam bursting off a doused fire, score climbing.
